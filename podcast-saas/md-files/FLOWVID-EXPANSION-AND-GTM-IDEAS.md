**Seven facts govern this volume** (each was independently re-discovered by two or three sweeps; stated once here so entries can stay short):

1. **Nothing FlowVid serves can be framed by a third party.** `shared/src/csp.ts` emits `frame-ancestors 'none'`, `client-web/next.config.ts` applies it to every path, `deploy/nginx/ssl-params.conf` adds `X-Frame-Options SAMEORIGIN`. Every embed, LMS, SCORM, MCP-Apps and fintech idea below routes through the `/embed` carve-out (V1 5.4 Frame Pass) — and `'embed'` is already pre-reserved in `backend-api/src/services/permalinkService.ts` `RESERVED_SLUGS`, so the codebase was expecting it. Sims themselves serve cross-origin from `sim-public.controller.ts` with a deploy-configurable `frame-ancestors browserOrigins()` — extending that allowlist is a config-shaped change, not a rewrite.
2. **There is no subscription, seat, or metered billing.** `BillingService` is one-time Stripe Checkout pay-to-unlock; `PLATFORM_FEE_PERCENT` defaults to 15; `creator_payout_cents` is bookkeeping with no Stripe Connect behind it. Every "$X/mo" idea below bills manually (Stripe links, invoices, pay-to-unlock) until V1 7.2 lands.
3. **There is no viewer identity.** Exactly one anonymous `share_token` per project (`schema.ts:174`), `branch_path_events.session_id` is client-minted and unlinkable. One bounded build — magic-link sign-in on share pages, server-issued sessions, `user_id` stamped into `branch_path_events` (insert at `branch.controller.ts:475`) — is the shared prerequisite under attestation, briefings, checkrides, consent records, LTI grading and per-patron analytics. Build it once (Bet 1), not six times.
4. **`sim_rum_events` is performance telemetry only** (`transition|residency|failure`, sampled, default-off, no project/user linkage — `shared/src/sim/rumEvents.ts:245`). Every comprehension claim below rides `branch_path_events` + `trigger_event`/`trigger_match` edges, where quiz-layout choice points (`ChoiceOverlay.tsx`) already record per-session answers today.
5. **Never promise burned-in MP4s.** The shipped export has no capture backend, so sim windows render as poster stills (`quality_state='degraded'`); the local capture provider is ~10x too slow on the 2-vCPU host (see the export-capture memories). Sell hosted interactive share links; the two honest attacks on the blocker are in Theme 7 (Creator-Side Render Farm, open-source SimCapture).
6. **No outbound email, no personal-access-token auth, no MCP dependency, no RSS** exist anywhere in `backend-api`. `api_keys` stores KMS-encrypted *LLM provider* keys (`ApiKeyService.ts`) — outward API auth is net-new. Every "weekly report email", "agent API" and "feed" claim below inherits these gaps.
7. **FlowVid does not generate simulations from prompts.** Sims are uploaded zip packages; the LLM writes the bridge, auto-scripts and guidance over them (`SimulationService`). The one text-to-sim lane is the avatar surface's single-file generator (`avatar/visualService.ts`). Every idea that said "the generator already builds X" was corrected against this fact.

## The strategy memo

*The wf3 strategic sweep produced a full memo rather than just a list. It is reproduced here in full (lightly copy-edited; paths made repo-relative) because its ordering logic — evidence-per-founder-week, paid time-boxed pilots, pre-registered success rules — is the frame the rest of this volume hangs on.*

### FlowVid Expansion Strategy — where validation gets deeper

**The question is not "where could the primitive go" (everywhere) but "which directions produce named-partner, paid, pre-registered evidence within a quarter — and leave usable rails behind if they fail."** Three bets clear that bar. Everything else is a door, a hedge, or a grave.

#### 1. The honest read of the research

Five findings decide the ordering:

1. **Interactivity as a format dies; interactivity bound to a measured outcome monetizes.** Every independent overlay company was absorbed into a platform (Wirewax→Vimeo, HapYak→Newsela→Brightcove, Rapt Media→Kaltura, Touchcast); Netflix decommissioned Bandersnatch as "limiting"; Eko found PMF only when interactivity served Walmart's conversion metric. FlowVid must sell the record of an outcome — an attestation, an intent signal, a comprehension delta — never the layer itself.
2. **The corporate measurement hole is real and budgeted.** Atlassian paid $975M for Loom while Atlassian-ecosystem content admits it "tracks views, not learning"; internal-email opens run 60–85% so opens prove nothing; policy tools attest "I read it," never "I understood it." Checkbox training is priced ($0.93–$2/seat at KnowBe4/NINJIO), a hand-built Storyline branching scenario costs $6–10K, and Ethena proves a two-person team can enter regulated training on content quality alone. This is the wedge with existing budget lines.
3. **Pedagogy without a buyer stays a grant.** PhET (~100M uses/yr) had its NSF POSE grant terminated April 2025; Distill went on hiatus over per-piece production cost; Nicky Case calls sustainability "50% luck." Money appears one layer up: Desmos sold the curriculum (→Amplify), Brilliant sells one owned stat (48% vs 4–10% completion), Synthesia passed $100M ARR selling to L&D. FlowVid's layer-up is the evidence record.
4. **Evidence is itself a purchasable product.** LearnPlatform sells "Evidence as a Service"; ESSA tiers now sit inside LAUSD and Chicago RFPs; only 26 of the top-100 edtech products have tier-aligned research; Bessemer's Jan-2026 vertical-AI bar is named design partners plus paid pilots. Validation machinery is product, not marketing.
5. **The repo already holds the rare asset — minus one piece.** Per-session decision paths (`branch_path_events` + trigger-edge sim events), avatar Q&A transcripts, and an aggregation endpoint ship today; competitors have view logs. The one missing piece everywhere is viewer identity. (Correctly noted across the lenses: `sim_rum_events` is performance telemetry only — every comprehension claim rides path events.)

Conclusion: the primitive wins first where a manipulation inside the sim IS the outcome someone already pays to prove. Order the bets by evidence-per-founder-week, and run every pilot as a paid, time-boxed charter with pre-registered success rules (the productized design-partner program) — that discipline is what makes the validation high-quality, not just deeper.

#### 2. The three bets, ranked

##### Bet 1 — Attested Understanding: the compliance-and-comms wedge
- **Thesis:** Where Loom tracks plays and attestation tools track clicks, a timestamped decision path of an employee performing the correct behavior in a simulation (quarantine the phish, route the reorg question) is categorically stronger evidence, sold into budgeted lines (KnowBe4-anchored per-seat; Staffbase-priced comms buyers who cannot show comprehension to leadership). Trigger-edge decision capture is wired end-to-end today; only identity is missing.
- **4-week experiment:** Two free pilots at once. (a) The simulated-inbox lesson runs as one Israeli startup's actual annual security training inside its SOC2 cycle, with a hand-rendered evidence pack from `branch_path_events`. (b) One real change announcement at a second company as a Role-Adaptive Briefing (pure existing machinery: cards choice point, per-role sequences, avatar Q&A), A/B'd against their all-staff email, with the per-role comprehension report handed to leadership.
- **Validated =** any two of: the auditor accepts the evidence pack; the report gets forwarded upward unprompted; a compliance manager quotes a seat price at or above KnowBe4's floor.
- **The one feature:** viewer identity — magic-link sign-in on share pages, server-issued session ids, and `user_id` stamped into `branch_path_events` (schema at `backend-api/src/db/schema.ts`, insert at `backend-api/src/controllers/v1/branch.controller.ts`). One bounded build shared by attestation, briefings, checkrides, consent, and LTI later.

##### Bet 2 — The Comprehension Delta Engine + four fake doors: evidence as the product
- **Thesis:** No pilot ends without a number nobody else can produce. LearnPlatform/ESSA prove evidence sells; Brilliant proves one owned stat compounds for years. The substrate (quiz choice points + path events) exists; fake doors with pre-registered thresholds turn vertical selection from opinion into behavior — the direct answer to "make validation deeper."
- **4-week experiment:** Week 1: migration adding a correct-answer flag to `branch_edges`, pre/post quiz checks on two flagship lessons, first delta computed by SQL. Weeks 2–4: four door mini-sites (consent, fintech-embed, devtools, research) reusing demo artifacts the now-track already produces, ~100 UTM-tagged targeted visitors per door, decision rule pre-registered: ≥3 pilot requests sends the design-partner push to that vertical.
- **Validated =** an honestly-n'd interaction→comprehension delta worth publishing (a null result is equally decision-grade — it says which sims teach), plus exactly one vertical clearing its door threshold.
- **The one feature:** the correctness flag + delta report script (SQL over `branch_path_events` plus one LLM theme pass over avatar transcripts, `ChoiceOverlay.tsx` quiz layout). Days of work; it instruments Bets 1 and 3.

##### Bet 3 — Hand-over-the-keys presales demos: the out-of-education proof
- **Thesis:** Demo automation is the richest adjacent pricing found (Consensus $600–1,250/mo, Navattic $500–1,000/mo) and splits cleanly into passive video vs unnarrated click-throughs; FlowVid's already-shipping mechanic — `auto_script` narration driving the sim, interaction-hold pausing when the buyer grabs it — is exactly the unbuilt fusion. A few presales wins validate the primitive entirely outside education, the strongest depth signal available for the platform thesis; interaction telemetry as lead scoring is an accepted motion (Instruqt's IQL, "3x qualified leads").
- **4-week experiment:** Day 1 dogfood — rebuild FlowVid's homepage hero as a narrated hand-over-the-keys lesson and publish it. Then build one free demo each for five presales leaders from PreSales Collective.
- **Validated =** two of five run it inside a live deal cycle and ask for the per-stakeholder engagement view by name.
- **The one feature:** a `share_links` table — multiple labeled per-recipient tokens per project (today exactly one `share_token` per project, `backend-api/src/db/schema.ts:174`). The CRM webhook waits.

**Calendar order inverts the rank:** Bet 2's MVP (days) lands first and instruments everything; Bet 1's identity build is weeks 1–2; Bet 3's dogfood runs day 1 in parallel. All three fit one 4-week window because only S/M-effort, verdict-now machinery was chosen.

#### 3. The graveyard, with the bodies named

- **Leading with the layer as the product** (Layer SDK, Kaltura campus plugin, YouTube pause-mode, .ivt-spec-first): the entire standalone-overlay category died into platforms — Wirewax, HapYak, Rapt, Touchcast, every one. Publish the .ivt spec later as a defensive hedge once a wedge pays; never as a validation bet.
- **Format-first interactivity** (Fork the Moment, Live Cohort Mode): Eko's "didn't catch on" era and Bandersnatch's decommissioning are the named warnings; both ideas also presume an audience FlowVid doesn't have yet.
- **Manipulable Podcasts:** the "existing podcast surface" is a private generator — no RSS, no public episode pages; smallest payer base of all 30+ ideas. Drop.
- **Museum kiosks:** no offline runtime exists anywhere in the tree; Intuiface owns per-device pricing; PhET's terminated grant shows how this adjacent world is funded. Drop.
- **Floor-Ready Safety Drills:** no scene generation exists in the repo; it duplicates Bet 1's attestation value at ~10x content cost through the founder's worst channel (on-site industrial sales). Drop.
- **Newsroom explainer desk:** Datawrapper at $599/yr and Flourish's free tier prove newsroom willingness-to-pay is rounding error; Distill died of exactly this production economics. At most one advocacy-flavored fake door.
- **Grant-cycle wallets now** (PolicyEngine civic explainers, ESG workshop kits): real rails, no paying customer inside a quarter. Later, behind doors only.
- **Parked, not buried — healthcare consent:** the best-priced vertical found (Emmi: $170M at >6x revenue, per-bed; EIDO in 65% of acute NHS trusts; teach-back −45% readmissions) but per-patient records make a solo founder a processor of identifiable medical data. It gets a door and a clinic LOI test; a clinic opening its own approval process is the go signal, never a patient pilot this quarter.

#### 4. Shared rails — no bet is stranded work

- **One event spine.** All three bets read the same `branch_path_events` + trigger-edge stream the education viewer already writes. Nothing forks the schema; a failed bet still leaves richer, better-tested telemetry under every education lesson.
- **Identity converts everywhere.** Bet 1's magic-link layer is also per-student progress for education cohorts, the LTI user mapping, per-rep checkrides, and eventual consent records. If compliance says no, education keeps the feature. Bet 3's per-recipient `share_links` are its anonymous cousin — classroom assignment links if presales says no.
- **The delta engine is the education core's own proof.** Pre/post checks on flagship lessons feed the Bakeoff and the ROI calculator, and the first published number is homepage material regardless of which vertical wins — the Brilliant play, run on owned telemetry.
- **Everything public rides the Library-share mini-site's rails.** The four doors, evidence pages, charter pages, and the presales dogfood are all published courses on the same `CoursePublishingService` / `SeoResolver` / `JsonLdService` / sitemap / OG / permalink machinery the in-flight mini-site uses. The mini-site gains every artifact as another indexed gallery page; the doors inherit its organic traffic; the doors' demo lessons are mini-site library content. One rail, compounding in both directions.
- **Constraints held everywhere:** no burned-in MP4 promises (the export-capture throughput blocker stands), SCORM stays a one-email kill-test on the agency channel rather than a build, and nothing above needs realtime, SSO, or subscription billing to reach its 4-week verdict — pilots bill through pay-to-unlock or manual Stripe links.

**Bottom line:** ship the delta engine this week, the identity layer next, the dogfood demo today; sell attested understanding first, evidence second, presales third; let four doors — not opinion — pick vertical number two.

## Theme 1 — Product-led growth loops

Every shared artifact, free tool and public page should acquire the next customer. The recurring correction from the code: the users table has no plan/tier field, so nothing can be "free tier shows it, paid removes it" yet — ship always-on and let badge-removal become the first reason a subscription exists.

### 1.1 "Made with FlowVid" badge on every public player
**What it is.** A small linked attribution chip on every public playback surface — `/v/[shareToken]`, `/[slug]` permalinks, `/c/[courseSlug]/[lessonSlug]` lessons, `/pl/[shareToken]` playlists — linking to a signup landing page with per-creator UTM attribution.
**Why it's good.** Verified: zero attribution exists anywhere in `client-web` today, so every share is a wasted impression — and share traffic is real (`share.controller.ts` increments `projects.view_count` per public load). Gamma attributes >50% of subscriber growth to its badge; Loom and Calendly made this the canonical B2B viral footer. Volume 1 rejected the "Made With Loop" version for needing a creator profile and a subscription to gate removal — this scoped-down always-on v1 needs neither, which is exactly why it survives.
**How it would work here.** One chip component inside `client-web/components/viewer/SharedViewerPage.tsx` (covers `/v/` and `/[slug]` automatically) plus the lesson footer in `app/c/[courseSlug]/[lessonSlug]/page.tsx` and the playlist viewer; link carries `utm_source=badge&utm_content={shareToken}`. No backend change for v1; `buildPlayerConfig.ts` grows a `hideBadge` flag when a plan model exists. Known co-fix: `/v/[shareToken]/page.tsx` has no `generateMetadata`, so the page the badge lives on unfurls blank — fix in the same pass.
**Effort.** S. **Verdict.** now. **First step.** Ship the badge and the `/v/` metadata together; both are the same afternoon.

### 1.2 Viewer-to-creator prompt: "Remake this lesson"
**What it is.** End-screen and pause-state CTAs on every shared lesson — "Remake this in your language", "Make one from YOUR notes" — pre-seeding project creation with the current lesson's `projects.topic`.
**Why it's good.** Loom's recipient→recorder loop aimed at viewers who are already the ICP, on pages that today dead-end with no ask at all. Two grounded corrections strengthen it: `/new` is a redirect stub, but `CreateProjectDialog.createProject({topic})` is a one-parameter call a home-page query param can seed; and anonymous Firebase auth is fully wired (`lib/firebase.ts` `signInAnonymously`, backend `is_anonymous`), so the viewer starts creating without a signup wall — signup happens when they want to keep the result, the highest-converting moment.
**How it would work here.** End-screen overlay in `SharedViewerPage.tsx` firing on player end and pause, linking to `/?topic={projects.topic}&utm_source=remake&utm_content={shareToken}`; `app/page.tsx` reads the param and opens `CreateProjectDialog` prefilled.
**Effort.** S. **Verdict.** now. **First step.** Add the overlay and the `useSearchParams` read; wire UTMs through.

### 1.3 Embeddable interactive player + oEmbed — the "Powered by" loop
*(merges the PLG sweep's "Embeddable player + oEmbed endpoint" and the B2B sweep's "Embeddable player with a Powered-by loop" — one route, two lenses; extends V1 5.4 Frame Pass with the acquisition mechanics)*
**What it is.** `/embed/[shareToken]` — an iframe embed of the full interactive lesson (video, chapters, live bridged sims) plus an oEmbed JSON route so pasted FlowVid links auto-expand in Notion, WordPress, Moodle; copy-embed-code in the share UI; the 1.1 badge non-removable inside embeds.
**Why it's good.** The embed is the demo: an interactive simulation inside a blog post screenshots badly, so people click it — the Loom physics claim holds. The hard cross-origin work is genuinely done: sim bundles serve cross-origin with helmet off, anonymous telemetry ingests with wildcard CORS, `share.controller.ts` already returns a paywall stub for locked content so paid embeds degrade gracefully, and `'embed'` is pre-reserved in `RESERVED_SLUGS`. The one real obstacle is deliberate (fact #1): the frame-ancestors carve-out must be scoped to `/embed/*` in both `next.config.ts` and nginx, and `e2e/production-smoke.spec.ts:48,89` updated knowingly.
**How it would work here.** New `client-web/app/embed/[shareToken]/page.tsx` reusing `SharedViewerPage` chrome-less with a postMessage resize handshake; per-route `headers()` override; oEmbed discovery route; `og:video`/`twitter:player` tags on share pages ride along (absorbing the useful half of the dropped animated-unfurls idea). Heavy player (HLS + sim pool) in third-party pages needs a perf pass.
**Effort.** S (route) → M (oEmbed + share UI). **Verdict.** now. **First step.** The route + header carve-out + one foreign-origin iframe playing a sim end to end — under a day, and it is also the foundation SCORM builds on (2.1).

### 1.4 Free tool pages: "turn any PDF / YouTube link into an interactive explainer"
**What it is.** No-signup pages at `/tools/*`: paste a URL or drop a PDF, get a shareable interactive lesson with one generated simulation; sign up to keep, edit or export. Scoped v1 = script + one interactive sim page, not a fully narrated video.
**Why it's good.** Highest-intent channel available (Veed's `/tools/` folder drives 3.5M+ monthly organic visits) and the ingestion pipeline genuinely exists end to end (`PDFIngester`/`YouTubeIngester`/`WebIngester` → `MarkItDownService` → `CorpusBuilder`). Anonymous Firebase auth means visitors get real user rows and existing per-user quotas apply. Corrections: the LLM chain is Anthropic→OpenAI→Gemini (no Groq provider in `services/llm/`); anonymous users are free to mint, so a per-IP cap beside `RateLimitService` is genuinely new abuse-control work; a full narrated run costs real TTS/b-roll money — the affordable wow is the generated sim via `avatar/visualService.ts`'s text-to-sim lane (fact #7).
**How it would work here.** Anonymous sign-in → project → ingest → summary script via `LLMService` (rolling `generation_daily_limit` + `cost_cents` recording bound spend) → one sim via the visualService path with library-first reuse → ordinary share token, so `/v/` pages and the badge just work. Add `'tools'` to `RESERVED_SLUGS`. The podcaster vertical folds in here as a `/tools/podcast-to-course` landing page (see the rejected "Podcast episodes → interactive editions").
**Effort.** L. **Verdict.** next. **First step.** Spike `/tools/youtube-to-lesson` through the existing corpus flow and read `cost_cents` off three runs to price the free tier.

### 1.5 Programmatic SEO: a page per simulation topic
**What it is.** Auto-generated public topic pages (`/simulations/photosynthesis`) each embedding a real sim, an excerpt, JSON-LD and a "generate your own" CTA, with Hebrew mirrors.
**Why it's good.** The SEO infrastructure is unusually ready — `SitemapService`, `JsonLdService`, `CanonicalUrlService`, ISR with a revalidate purge hook, and `SlugService` really does transliterate Hebrew (`HEBREW_MAP` is in the code); `projects` even has `topic`/`seo_description`/`seo_keywords` columns. What is not ready is inventory: pSEO ranks on breadth and the public sim corpus is a handful of packages. Launching 20 thin pages does nothing; 500 needs the free-tools engine (1.4) generating the corpus first. Right playbook (Canva: ~21K template pages → 13.1M visits/mo), wrong month.
**How it would work here.** Topic-page template on the `/c/` stack over distinct `projects.topic`; the live sim is the `/embed` route (1.3); add `'simulations'` to `RESERVED_SLUGS` (a creator could claim it today).
**Effort.** M. **Verdict.** later (sequenced behind 1.3 + 1.4). **First step.** One query counting distinct non-null `projects.topic` among shared projects, to size real inventory.

### 1.6 Community gallery with "Remix this lesson"

## Theme 6 — Open-source & ecosystem

What to open, under which license, and how each opening acquires users or builds moat. The dividing line, stated once: formats, runtimes, validators, capture tooling and distribution shims open (openness compounds); the LLM orchestration and repair prompts, canary thresholds, avatar assets, billing and the hosted SaaS stay closed (that is the margin). Remotion's terms (free ≤3 employees, company license above) are the template — set commercial terms on day one; tldraw's MIT-then-relicense backlash is the cautionary tale.

### 6.1 OpenSim Package Spec v1 — publish the sim artifact format
**What it is.** Extract the sim package format from `shared/src/sim` into a public Apache-2.0 spec repo + `@flowvid/sim-spec` npm package: a portable, hash-verified format for sandboxed interactive simulations, with the exhaustive reference validator. The prose is largely already written — the doc comments in `simManifest.ts` ("hashes are of final bytes, always", "validation is part of the type") read like a spec draft.
**Why it's good.** Whoever writes the format for AI-generated educational sims sets the standard — H5P became the default interactive-content format for every LMS exactly this way, and `.h5p` is static-widgets-only, leaving the sim slot open. A published spec turns every "can I get my sims out?" procurement objection into a yes and makes third-party tools produce FlowVid-playable inventory. The repo check confirms the cheapest credible opening: `shared/` already exposes a `./sim/*` subpath export map, the types are zod-free and dependency-light (`sha256.ts` is the only runtime dep), and `validateManifest` returns every problem rather than the first — genuinely spec-grade.
**How it would work here.** Scaffold `packages/sim-spec` from `simManifest/simIdentity/simPolicy/simRevision/sha256` + tests; move platform-coupled fields (projectId, simulationId, storagePrefix) behind an `x-flowvid` extension block — real design work, not copy-paste; declare OpenSim v1 = `SIM_MANIFEST_VERSION` 1 with v3 (`runtimeProtocol.ts`) as the spec'd runtime contract and v2 documented as legacy-compat; repoint backend/client imports at the published package so the spec cannot drift. Once FlowVid consumes it, breaking changes need public versioning discipline forever.
**Effort.** M. **Verdict.** now. **First step.** Scaffold the package, make `pnpm test` green against the built output, and write the one-page public-vs-extension field inventory — the go/no-go artifact.

**What it is.** A ministry studio generating each week's kids/youth lesson from the pastor's actual sermon: narrated story video, one playable "parable sim," printable discussion cards, a volunteer teacher script — so the 11am kids room finally mirrors the adult series.
**Why it's good.** Churches pay Orange $525–2,859/yr for packaged curriculum that never matches the local sermon series — a complaint every family pastor voices — and lessons are delivered by untrained volunteers who need scripts. At $99/mo FlowVid undercuts Orange while offering the one thing Orange structurally cannot: your sermon becomes Sunday's lesson. ~300K US Protestant congregations; churches renew reliably and buy on relationship — ideal for one lighthouse megachurch. Corrections: the theology gate is the course draft→published review (the pitched "GuidanceService review flow" was a misread); no print output exists; no RSS feed, so the "midweek devotional feed" is a link. The operational risk is the weekly Sunday-morning SLA on a generation pipeline whose failures become the founder's Saturday night.
**Effort.** M. **Verdict.** later. **First step.** One real sermon manuscript through `DocumentIngester` → a kids-lesson project with one parable sim → the share link to one family pastor with the question "would you put this on the screen Sunday?"

### 8.8 Field Trip Factory (museum & science-center education departments)
**What it is.** A museum's education department turns each exhibit into a sellable virtual field trip — pre-visit interactive lesson, in-gallery phone companion, post-visit check — generated from exhibit copy, catalogs and a curator interview, sold to schools at the museum's existing $200–300 program price with zero live staffing.
**Why it's good.** Education departments already sell virtual programs (Cranbrook $200, Pacific Science Center $250+, Catawba $6/student) but every delivery is a live Zoom consuming scarce educator hours, while custom digital interactives from exhibit firms run $20K–150K per station. The money is often earmarked: IMLS and Digital Museums Canada grants explicitly fund educational digitization — a $499/mo institution plan against a $20K kiosk quote is an easy board approval. (Unlike the *dropped* physical-kiosk idea, everything here is the normal online product.) Gaps: no time-boxed access (purchases are permanent, tokens never expire), no graded assessment (quiz table is Phase 4), and committee/grant sales cycles measured in quarters.
**Effort.** M. **Verdict.** later. **First step.** Rebuild one exhibit page via `WebIngester` into a pre-visit lesson with one sim, publish as a public course page (JSON-LD for free), cold-email the link to one science-center education director.

### 8.9 FlowVid Prime — the Nebula×Brilliant network — audacious repositioning
**What it is.** Flip from selling the tool to owning the network: an $8–12/mo consumer subscription bundling the best creator-made interactive courses, with 50% of revenue pooled to creators by engaged time (Nebula's exact mechanic) — every creator a recruiting channel for a user-generated Brilliant competitor.
**Why it's good.** Brilliant proves consumers pay $161.88/yr for interactive STEM lessons yet ships only ~90 in-house courses after a decade (~$14.3M revenue) because hand-built interactivity doesn't scale; Nebula proves 175 creators cross-promoting built 650K+ subscribers at $6/mo with churn falling to 4.4%. FlowVid's pipeline is the missing piece both lack: catalog breadth at near-zero marginal cost, marketed by creators to their own audiences. "Mechanically modest" undersells the gap: the catalog side is real (`publish_state` + an opt-in flag + the public course query/SEO stack), but everything monetary is not — no consumer subscriptions, no recurring entitlements, no payout ledger, no creator payout rails, and engaged-time allocation has no telemetry substrate (fact #4). The supply gate is the right discipline: 200+ high-quality opted-in courses do not exist. Sequence strictly behind 8.1, 1.8 and the per-viewer analytics.
**Effort.** XL. **Verdict.** later. **First step.** Run one query — published courses with `publish_state='published'` — and write a one-page ADR for the opt-in flag and payout-ledger schema; the count against the 200-course gate makes the sequencing self-evident.

# Market notes

The sharpest named findings from the strategic and niche sweeps' research, condensed. Keep the numbers; they are the price anchors every entry above sells against.

### The corporate measurement hole (internal comms & training)
- **Atlassian paid $975M for Loom** (25M users, 200K paying customers) — and Atlassian-ecosystem content admits it "tracks views, not learning." Internal-email opens run 60–85%, so opens prove nothing; Staffbase/Firstup are quote-only at $10K+/yr and their buyers' loudest complaint is that they cannot show comprehension to leadership.
- **Synthesia** enterprise deals run $25–40K/yr (Vendr median ~$30K), gate SCORM behind Enterprise, and charge $1,000/yr per custom avatar; **Colossyan** wins deals against it selling branching + quizzes at $59/mo — "interactivity cheap" is the working wedge, but nobody in that lane can generate a functional simulation of the thing being taught.
- **Sales enablement is priced for pain:** Highspot average contract $91,460/yr; Mindtickle ~$92K; AI roleplay (Second Nature ~$20K minimum, Hyperbound ~$15K/yr) sells only conversation practice, not an operable model of the product or pricing.
- **Compliance is commoditized at the content layer:** KnowBe4 Compliance Plus from $0.93/seat/mo; NINJIO ~$2/user/mo for one monthly episode + quiz; **Ethena** (G2 4.6; Netflix/Figma/Zendesk) entered as a two-person team on content quality alone. Policy-attestation tools ($79–139/mo up to PolicyTech) record "I read it," never understanding.
- **One hand-built Storyline branching scenario = 80 in-house / 120+ contractor hours ($6–10K)** on top of a $499–649/yr license — the strongest single cost anchor for generated simulations. Vendor tabletop exercises: $15–25K per half-day. Strivr-style VR safety: $200–1,000/employee/yr, pilots $75–150K. WalkMe median contract $43.8K/yr with chronic implementation complaints. Jellyvision ALEX proves "an interactive conversation about YOUR numbers" is a real enterprise category, sold through benefits brokers.
- **M&A:** 47% of key employees leave within a year of a deal; McKinsey attributes ~50% of merger failures to organizational/communication issues; no product exists for "explain this specific integration interactively per role."

### The overlay graveyard and the open flank (platform)
- **YouTube's Required Minimum Functionality terms flatly ban overlays** in front of any part of an embedded player — Edpuzzle's pause-beside-the-player pattern (confirmed compliant by their own help center) is the only legal shape, and it carried them to a $13.50/mo teacher business.
- **The standalone overlay category died completely:** Wirewax→Vimeo (now an Enterprise-only add-on), HapYak→Newsela→Brightcove (marketing discontinued), Rapt Media→Kaltura (no longer independently developed), Touchcast→abandoned interactivity. **Eko** (Sequoia/Intel/MGM-backed) found PMF only in Walmart's product galleries where interactivity served a conversion metric; **Netflix decommissioned Bandersnatch** calling the tech "limiting." Interactivity as a format dies; bound to a measured outcome, it monetizes.
- **The edu rails are weak in documented ways:** Cornell's teaching center records that Kaltura and Panopto in-video quizzes are multiple-choice only; H5P's xAPI is inconsistent (no completion event without a submit screen; statements unreachable in iframes); **Annoto** proves you can sell a layer riding Kaltura's player as a plugin without hosting a byte of video.
- **Pricing found:** Mindstamp $29–249/mo; Hihaho €97/video; ThingLink $25–450/mo; Firework ~$259/mo per 50K views; vs demo automation an order of magnitude up — **Consensus $600–1,250/mo, Navattic $500–1,000/mo** — split into passive video vs unnarrated click-throughs with nobody fusing narration and hand-over-the-keys control.
- **In developer land there is a genuine vacuum:** Media Chrome stops at player controls, videojs-overlay is stale, projectorjs abandoned — no maintained open interaction-layer engine and no open format for the interactivity track, even though WebVTT metadata cues can already carry time-aligned JSON and xAPI video profile + cmi5 + LTI 1.3 provide finished launch/tracking/grading rails. **Kyron Learning** ($15.5M, Gates money) attacks from the conversation side and owns no manipulable state. PhET's historical sims are CC-BY including commercial use — legal seed content for a sim-over-video engine.

### Evidence, verticals and validation (strategy)
- **Interactive sim content is pedagogically proven and institutionally unmonetized:** PhET (~100M uses/yr) lost its $1.5M NSF POSE grant April 2025; Distill went on hiatus because each piece needed a viz expert + web dev + subject specialist — precisely the team the pipeline replaces; Nicky Case calls sustainability "50% luck." Money appears one layer up: Desmos sold the curriculum (→Amplify); **Brilliant charges $24.99/mo on one owned stat (~48% completion vs 4–10% for video MOOCs)**; Synthesia passed $100M ARR selling to L&D.
- **Healthcare is the best-priced vertical:** Wolters Kluwer paid **$170M (>6x revenue) for Emmi**, billed per hospital bed; EIDO's consent content sits in 65% of acute NHS trusts covering 8M consent decisions/yr; Washington State runs the only US decision-aid certification (IPDAS-based) with heightened legal protection for certified aids; teach-back shows −45% 30-day readmissions; 30.4% of spinal malpractice claims allege failure to explain risks.
- **Evidence generation is purchasable:** LearnPlatform sells "Evidence as a Service"; ESSA tiers are written into LAUSD and Chicago RFPs; only 26 of the top-100 edtech products have tier-aligned research; Bessemer's Jan-2026 vertical-AI fundability bar = named design partners + paid pilots. **En-ROADS** (1.5M users, 23 languages; a 2026 npj Climate Action study showing significant decision-maker gains) shows the facilitated-workshop distribution model.
- Other anchors: SundaySky sells personalized (non-interactive) video on enterprise pricing claiming up to 40% call reduction; government rules engines are free APIs (PolicyEngine → MyFriendBen in four states) with Social Pinpoint at 700 gov customers; journals price the per-artifact market (AIP $1,100, Research Square/Wiley $1,500 per video abstract); Instruqt invented the "Instruqt-Qualified Lead" claiming 3x more qualified leads; Vector Solutions ~$4K/yr per ~75 employees for safety libraries.

### Open source, agents and the 2026 stack (ecosystem & frontier)
- **Remotion** is the closest precedent: source-available, free ≤3 employees, then $25/seat/mo or $0.01/render ($100/mo minimum); 32K GitHub stars by late Jan 2026; its Claude Code skill hit **6M views in 48 hours and 150K installs on skills.sh within eight weeks** — agent-facing distribution is the 2026 Show HN. **tldraw** is playbook and warning at once: Make Real went viral partly because the starter was open at peak attention, and the MIT→license-key pivot triggered a community fork — set commercial terms on day one. **H5P**: open format + open player, paid hosting (H5P.com with LTI into every LMS); its content types are static widgets, leaving generated sandboxed sims an open flank. **Obsidian**: ~$25M ARR with 7 people on 1,000+ community plugins — the ecosystem moat, post-PMF only. **Plausible** $1M ARR bootstrapped; **Cal.com** concedes self-hosters never pay but put you on enterprise radar; **Manim**'s community fork warns: open only contribution surfaces you can maintain.
- **MCP:** 5,415 companies had servers by Aug 19, 2026 (Stripe, HubSpot, Twilio, Amazon, Google, Meta, TikTok); Gartner projects 40% of enterprise apps embedding task-specific agents by end-2026; Mini Course Generator's "first agentic LMS" MCP returns flip cards — the working-sim slot is empty. **MCP Apps** (Jan 26, 2026, Anthropic + OpenAI) renders tool-returned HTML in sandboxed iframes inside Claude/ChatGPT/VS Code/Goose. **A2A v1.0** is stable under the Linux Foundation (150+ orgs). The **Atlas retirement** (Aug 9, 2026; AI browsers projected at 1–3% share) says distribution materialized inside chat clients, not new browsers.
- **The client GPU became a platform:** Safari 26 shipped WebGPU (Sept 15, 2025) completing 90%+ desktop coverage; Chrome 148 stabilized the Prompt API with Gemini Nano (Trip.com runs client-side inference in production); WebLLM hits ~41 tok/s on Llama-8B; **kokoro-js** runs the #1-ranked 82M TTS free in-browser; Whisper-base transcribes in-browser with 5–10x WebGPU speedup. **Fish Audio** S1 at $15/M bytes with 10–30s cloning undercuts ElevenLabs 3.3–6.7x; **Anam** (already wired in) charges $0.15–0.24/min, #1 on the 2026 Avatar Benchmark; HeyGen ~$1–4/min. **Gemini Live** went GA (~$0.005/min audio in — ~32x cheaper on input than OpenAI Realtime), making an always-on seeing tutor economically sane. **SAM 3** (Nov 19, 2025) does promptable concept segmentation with video tracking; **SPZ** open-sourced ("the .jpg for splats", 8–12x compression) with free Scaniverse capture; **TRELLIS.2** (MIT) and Hunyuan3D 3.5 do single-image→3D in under a minute; **LTX-2** open-sourced (free commercial <$10M ARR); Decart's MirageLSD restyles live streams under 40ms/frame. World models (Genie 3 / Project Genie, Odyssey-2) generate navigable worlds but "cannot implement game mechanics" — wow without assessability, which is exactly the flank the contract-verified coded sim defends. Anthropic's structured outputs (Nov 14, 2025, constrained decoding) let zod-validated pipeline stages drop their JSON-retry class entirely.

### Adjacent-market price anchors (creator economy and beyond)
- **YouTube educators:** $3–15 AdSense CPMs vs $15–80 sponsorship; Memberships take 30% vs Patreon ~10%; they already pay TubeBuddy/vidIQ $15–99/mo. **Nebula:** 650K+ subscribers, 175 creators, $6/mo, 50% of revenue distributed by watch time, churn cut from 9% to 4.4%. **Brilliant:** $27.99/mo / $161.88/yr, ~$14.3M revenue 2024, ~10M users, only ~90 hand-built courses — the catalog bottleneck generation removes.
- **Test prep:** UWorld $559/yr (Step 1) on explanation reputation; AMBOSS $8.25–14.99/mo; Manhattan Prep $300–2,900; Israeli psychometric institutes sell four-figure courses. **Courseware:** MyLab/Mastering codes ~$33–181/student/course; Labster ~$79–109/student. **Maven:** 10% take, instructors keep 90%, ~$20K average cohort.
- **Tutoring take-rates hurt:** Wyzant keeps 25% (+9% student fee; ~$26.83/hr average); Preply 33%→18% and 100% of trials; Outschool 30% — take-rate pain makes tutors receptive to owned-brand tools, but marketplace ToS forbid the off-platform loop (why Session Echo died).
- **Homeschool/ESA:** ~$8.65B market; $1,200–2,500 per child per year; 101,914 ESA accounts by Jan 2026 (~10x since 2022) at $7–8K/student; 38% of microschools accept ESA funds. **Church curriculum:** Orange 252 Kids $525–2,859/yr across 10,000+ churches; SermonCentral PRO $119.50/yr. **Museums:** live virtual programs at $200–250 (or $6/student) vs $20K–150K custom interactives; IMLS and Digital Museums Canada (≤$250K/project) fund digitization. **Webinars:** ~half of views are on-demand yet 89% of webinar leads come from live attendance; 65% of B2B marketers repurpose webinars. **Substack:** $450M gross writer revenue 2025, 5M+ paid subscriptions, 50+ writers at $1M+ ARR concentrated in explainer-shaped niches. **Keynotes:** ~$15.5K average ($26.6K corporate), nothing earned after the talk. **Continuing education:** $66.9B (2024) → ~$96B by 2030 at $10–60/credit-hour. **Agency explainer production — the cost FlowVid collapses:** $3–10K per finished minute, median custom 60-second quote ~$9,680.
